# AI copilots for the physical world.

I'm Sebastián Rojo, CTO and co-founder of [EmiliaVision](https://emiliavision.com), where we are building real-time AI copilots for frontline teams.

Security cameras already see what happens in the physical world. Emilia turns those existing signals into understanding, timely events, and useful action. We are starting with restaurants, augmenting the people who keep them running rather than trying to replace them.

We currently work with 14 international customers operating more than 500 locations. The challenge now is to take Emilia from a fast-moving MVP to a reliable platform that can scale from 1 to 100.

## The work

The physical world is a hostile environment for software. Networks disappear. Cameras move. Hardware behaves differently in every location. Reality refuses to match the training data.

That makes the work interesting.

We build across computer vision, AI agents, real-time video and data pipelines, edge systems, and cloud infrastructure. A promising model or polished demo is only the beginning—the system has to produce useful outcomes every day in real businesses.

## How I build

I like small teams with high trust, short feedback loops, and end-to-end ownership.

- Start from the real problem, not the fashionable technology.
- Find the shortest path to evidence.
- Prototype quickly, measure what happens, and then harden the system.
- Use AI as leverage, not as a substitute for judgment.
- Own the complete loop: problem, code, deployment, observation, and user outcome.
- Move fast without gambling with production.
- Make failures visible and turn them into better systems.

> Turn ambiguity into experiments, experiments into shipped systems, and failures into better judgment.

## Builders I want to work with

I care less about pedigree than about slope and evidence.

You do not need a perfect résumé, a traditional career path, or prior experience with every part of our stack. I am interested in what you have built, automated, repaired, reverse-engineered, contributed to, or placed in front of real users.

The people I work best with are curious enough to inspect the layer below the abstraction, resourceful enough to keep moving when information is incomplete, and responsible enough to know that hacker mindset is not an excuse for reckless engineering.

In return, Emilia offers broad ownership, direct access to founders and users, difficult real-world problems, fast feedback, and the opportunity to influence both architecture and engineering culture.
